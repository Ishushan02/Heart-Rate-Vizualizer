The Project

Understanding the heart
through interactive visualization

Cardiac conditions affect millions worldwide, yet the inner workings of the heart remain opaque to most patients, students, and even clinicians outside cardiology. Our project bridges that gap with a real-time, interactive visualization tool.

🫀

Problem Space

Clinical EKG readings are abstract waveforms that are difficult to interpret without years of training. Patients receiving cardiac diagnoses often cannot visualize what is happening inside their heart, leading to poor health literacy and treatment adherence. Educational tools that exist today are static, 2D, and disconnected from real physiological timing.

💡

Solution Space

CardioView renders a fully animated 3D anatomical heart chambers, great vessels, and coronary arteries synchronized frame-by-frame to a PQRST EKG waveform. Users input any heart rate (30–220 BPM) and watch the cardiac cycle unfold in real time: atrial kick, ventricular systole, ejection, and diastolic filling, all visible from any angle via 3D rotation.

🎯

Why This Approach

Simpler 2D animations fail to convey spatial relationships between chambers and vessels. A generative/procedural 3D model (Three.js) with physics-based lighting and particle-based blood flow is necessary because no pre-built static model can respond dynamically to arbitrary BPM inputs while preserving anatomical accuracy across all viewing angles.

Methodology

How we're refining
the solution

Our development follows an iterative, human-centered design process, combining technical prototyping with clinical and educational feedback.

01

Mathematical Modeling of the Cardiac Cycle

We model the PQRST waveform using Gaussian basis functions, with each cardiac phase (P wave, QRS complex, T wave) precisely parameterized. All 3D animations derive directly from this model ensuring physiological accuracy at any BPM.

02

Anatomical Prototyping with 3D Web Technologies

Using Three.js, we procedurally generate chambers (LV, RV, LA, RA), great vessels (aorta, pulmonary trunk, SVC, IVC), pulmonary veins, and coronary arteries (LAD, RCA, LCx) as parametric tube geometries enabling rotation, zoom, and inspection from any angle.

03

Physician & Educator Feedback

We plan structured interviews with cardiologists and medical educators to validate anatomical accuracy, correct vessel positioning, and assess whether the visualization aids clinical explanation. Feedback will inform geometry refinement and label placement.

04

Patient-Facing Usability Testing

Usability studies with non-clinical participants will evaluate comprehension: can a patient understand their arrhythmia or tachycardia by interacting with the tool? We will use think-aloud protocols and pre/post knowledge assessments.

05

Integration & Iteration

Based on feedback, we will add valve animations, pathology modes (AFib, bradycardia, tachycardia waveforms), and potentially connect to real BPM data from wearables via the Web Bluetooth API for live patient monitoring scenarios.
